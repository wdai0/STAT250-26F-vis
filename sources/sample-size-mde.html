Both population means stay fixed. The curves describe sample means across repeated studies; they get narrower because SE = σ/√n.

Power is the probability of rejecting H₀ when a specified alternative is true. Here that alternative always has mean 0.5, and the null mean always equals zero. Only the sample size changes.

The numerical cutoff changes with n, so the null probability to its right remains 5%. Keeping α fixed does not mean keeping this sample-mean cutoff fixed.

SE = 2 / √n
c = z0.95 SE
β = Φ((c − 0.5) / SE)
Power = 1 − β = Φ(0.5 / SE − z0.95)

Interpret the probabilities

Power is calculated under the fixed alternative, before a study's result is observed. It is not the probability that the alternative is true.

Alpha and beta belong to different hypothetical population realities; they are not complementary pieces of one curve. Under the fixed alternative, beta and power do sum to one.

A statistically detectable effect need not be practically important. Increasing sample size reduces random variation; it does not automatically remove bias or establish causality.

Related question: minimum detectable effect

MDE fixes a target power, then finds the smallest positive true effect that meets that target. At the current sample size, the MDE for 80% power is outcome units.

δmin = (z0.95 + z0.80) × 2 / √n

That is a different calculation: it selects a different alternative as n changes. The main plots always use the true effect 0.5 and allow power to change.

“Minimum” is relative to the chosen 80% target. Smaller effects can still produce a significant result, but with less than 80% probability; an effect at the target can still be missed in 20% of repeated studies.

Model assumptions. Independent normal observations, unknown mean μ, known population standard deviation σ = 2 outcome units. Test H₀: μ = 0 against Hₐ: μ > 0; reject when the sample mean exceeds c. The sample size counts original independent observations in one sample, not observations per group or simulation repetitions.

Under mean μ, the sample mean is normal with mean μ and standard deviation 2/√n. The fixed positive effect is μ₁ − μ₀ = 0.5 outcome units. The cutoff uses z0.95 = 1.6448536269514722; the optional MDE calculation also uses z0.80 = 0.8416212335729143.

If α stays at 5%, why can power increase?

Alpha is a probability under the null; power is a probability under the fixed alternative. As sample means become more precise, we can retain a 5% false-alarm rate while detecting the same real effect more often.
