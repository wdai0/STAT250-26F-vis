Inference with a normal distribution

Same inference question, two ways to represent sampling variation.

The study: use of a streaming app’s offline downloads

A streaming app lets paid subscribers download movies and shows to watch without an internet connection. The company wants to know what proportion of all its paid subscribers used this offline-download feature in the past 30 days. This unknown population proportion is denoted by p.

In this hypothetical study, a simple random sample of 100 paid subscribers is asked: “Have you used the app’s offline-download feature in the past 30 days?” Each subscriber answers “yes” or “no”. Assume all selected subscribers respond, and the sample is small enough relative to the subscriber population that responses can be treated as independent.

In the observed sample, 60 subscribers answer “yes” and 40 answer “no”. The sample proportion is therefore p̂ = 60/100 = 0.60. We know that 60% of these sampled subscribers used the feature in the past 30 days; we do not know the proportion among all paid subscribers. A different random sample could give a different sample proportion.

Hypothesis testing: assess a claim about the population.
If exactly 50% of all paid subscribers used offline downloads in the past 30 days, how unusual would a sample result at least as far from 50% as the observed 60% be?
Confidence intervals: estimate the population proportion.
Using the observed sample, what interval can we construct to estimate the proportion of all paid subscribers who used offline downloads in the past 30 days, at a chosen confidence level?

Both views use this same study and reference sample. Within each view, we compare a simulation-based calculation with a normal approximation.

We have not changed what a p-value or confidence interval means. We have changed how we approximate the sampling variation used to calculate it.
