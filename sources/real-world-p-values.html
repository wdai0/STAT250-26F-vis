Business · Nature · 2024

Hybrid working from home improves retention without damaging performance

Nicholas Bloom, Ruobing Han & James Liang · Nature 630, 920–925.

Read the paper ↗ DOI ↗ Result in PDF, p. 2 ↗

01 / Read the published result

A six-month randomized study of 1,612 Trip.com employees compared two home-working days per week with full-time office attendance.

Exact p-value excerpt
“P = 0.043”
Authors’ conclusion · exact sentence from the abstract
“We found that hybrid working improved job satisfaction and reduced quit rates by one-third.”

The full p-value sentence appears under “Effects on employee retention,” PDF p. 2 (printed p. 921). Figure 2 identifies a two-tailed t-test.
Read the complete result sentence ↗

02 / Interpret it · instructor wording

The null claim

The underlying quit rates are equal for the hybrid and office groups.

The observed result

Quit rates were 4.8% versus 7.2%: 2.4 percentage points lower in the hybrid group, a one-third relative reduction.

What p = 0.043 means here

If the underlying quit rates were equal, and the test assumptions held, a standardized difference at least this extreme in either direction would occur about 4.3% of the time.

Keep the outcome in view. This p-value concerns quitting; it does not test every performance claim in the title.

Study overview · paraphrase

The trial examined hybrid work, satisfaction, and retention, with additional analyses of performance and promotions. Managers became more positive about hybrid work. This is a condensed summary, not the original abstract.

Physics · Physics Letters B · 2012

Observation of a new particle in the search for the Standard Model Higgs boson with the ATLAS detector at the LHC

ATLAS Collaboration · Physics Letters B 716, 1–29.

Read the paper ↗ DOI ↗ Conclusion in PDF, p. 21 ↗

01 / Read the published result

ATLAS combined collision measurements from several detection channels to search for an additional particle signal beyond known background processes.

Exact p-value excerpt · local significance
“p₀ = 1.7 × 10⁻⁹”
Authors’ conclusion · exact sentence fragment
“These results provide conclusive evidence for the discovery of a new particle”

Both complete sentences appear in Section 10, “Conclusion,” PDF p. 21 (printed manuscript p. 20). Section 7 explains the statistical interpretation.
Read the complete result sentence ↗

02 / Interpret it · instructor wording

The null claim

Background processes alone explain the measurements, without the additional particle signal.

The observed result

An excess near 126 GeV had a reported local significance of 5.9 standard deviations.

What p₀ = 1.7 × 10⁻⁹ means here

Under the background-only model and its assumptions, an excess at least this significant at the specified mass would have probability approximately 1.7 × 10⁻⁹.

Keep the model in view. This is a local p-value, not the probability that the particle does not exist.

Study overview and local versus global significance

Overview — paraphrase: The combined measurements supported a new neutral particle compatible with the Standard Model Higgs boson. Detailed particle identification required more data.

Instructor note: Accounting for the search across 110–600 GeV gives a global p-value of 1.7 × 10⁻⁷. The local value above addresses the specified mass; the global value accounts for searching a range.

Survodutide Once Weekly for the Treatment of Adults with Obesity

Carel W. le Roux et al., SYNCHRONIZE-1 Investigators · NEJM 395, 776–787.
Published online June 7, 2026 · Issue date August 20, 2026.

01 / Read the published result

A double-blind randomized trial assigned 725 adults without diabetes to two survodutide doses or placebo, alongside lifestyle counseling, for 76 weeks.

Exact p-value excerpt
“P<0.001 for all comparisons with placebo”
Authors’ conclusion · exact sentence from the abstract
“Survodutide led to significantly greater reductions in body weight than placebo in adults with obesity without diabetes.”

See the abstract’s Results paragraph and the immediately following Conclusions paragraph. The Results sentence includes mean weight changes and proportions losing at least 5%.
Read the complete result sentence ↗

02 / Interpret it · instructor wording

The null claim

There is no treatment difference for a tested drug–placebo comparison.

The observed result

Mean weight reductions were 12.2% (3.6 mg), 13.0% (6.0 mg), and 5.4% (placebo) at 76 weeks.

What p < 0.001 means here

For a tested drug–placebo comparison, assuming no treatment difference and the analysis assumptions, a test statistic at least this extreme would have probability below 0.1%.

Keep the bound in view. This summarizes multiple comparisons, not one pooled p-value. “Less than 0.001” is a bound, not an exact value; efficacy does not establish safety.

Study overview and analysis limits

Overview — paraphrase: Weight reductions favored treatment; gastrointestinal adverse events were more frequent with treatment.

The primary explanation here uses mean weight reduction. The abstract also reports the proportion losing at least 5%. The protocol’s detailed multiplicity procedure has not been independently audited for this teaching example.

03 / The common statistical idea

Assume the null. Then ask how unusual the result is.

Assuming the null hypothesis and statistical model are correct, the p-value is the probability of obtaining a test statistic as extreme as, or more extreme than, the one observed.

Null claim→Reference distribution under the null→As extreme or more extreme→Tail probability

“Extreme” follows the direction or directions specified by the test: unusually high, unusually low, or far from the null in either direction. It need not mean a larger raw measurement.

Where does a threshold fit?

A significance level α is chosen before examining results. For the classroom rule here, p ≤ α → reject H₀; p > α → fail to reject H₀.

Under the null and a valid testing procedure, α limits the long-run probability of a Type I error: rejecting a true null.

A cutoff changes the decision rule, not the p-value. Values such as 0.049 and 0.051 do not represent sharply different scientific evidence.

Apply a hypothetical rule to the selected paper’s reported value. This does not reconstruct that study’s decision protocol, discipline-specific standards, or multiplicity adjustments.

What the p-value does not tell us

  • It is not the probability that H₀ is true, or that chance caused the result.
  • It is not effect size, practical importance, safety, or the probability of replication.
  • A large p-value does not establish no effect; a small one does not validate the study’s assumptions.
  • Do not rank the importance of these studies by their p-values.
Teaching notes, source details, and mathematical form

Reusable interpretation: If [null claim in context] were true, and [model and design assumptions] held, then [a test statistic at least as extreme in the specified direction or directions] would occur with probability [reported p-value or bound].

Connection to randomization: The course simulation estimates a tail probability by counting qualifying simulated statistics. Published analyses may use analytical approximations or more elaborate models; these papers did not necessarily use the course’s simulation algorithm.

When larger T means a stronger departure: p = PrH₀(T ≥ tobs).

For a two-sided test, use the test’s specified extremeness rule; do not automatically double a tail for every model. Handling nuisance parameters in a composite null is beyond this introductory explanation.
